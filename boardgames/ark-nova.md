# Ark Nova (with Marine Worlds)

Played on Board Game Arena, usually 2-player against The Professor. Compiled 2026-11-16 from the coaching chats of Oct 22 to Nov 16.

## Before giving any advice

- Ask for a screenshot instead of guessing map layout, money, hand or card costs.
- Read the card text. If an icon or number isn't legible, say so and ask Andrew to hover it. Do not fill gaps from memory.
- Plan the whole turn sequence (which action moves to which slot), not one card at a time.

## Rules Claude got wrong

- **Icons under a card's cost are play requirements**, not icons the card gives. The Lynx needs 2 Europe icons already in the zoo.
- **Continent icons were misread** (the Reindeer is Americas, not Europe). Confirm the icon before building a plan on it.
- **Yosemite's tiers count animal size**, not number of icons.
- **Water adjacency**: some builds and animals require it; check before recommending a placement.
- **Longcomb Sawfish needs a sea animal already in the zoo.**
- **Enclosure sizes were misread** (Bald Eagle). Read the size off the card.
- **Extra Shift** was explained wrong; read the bonus text.
- **Invented rules**: a discount that didn't exist, a strength rule from a different game, "the aquarium is full" when it wasn't, and a wrong cost for a Clever animal. If a rule isn't on the card or in the rulebook, don't state it.
- **Action card text** (Boost, multipliers, strength thresholds on alternate action cards) was misread several times. Re-read the actual card each game; the Marine Worlds alternates differ.
- **A standard enclosure holds one animal.** Put a big animal in a big enclosure; don't spend build strength on an enclosure smaller than the strength allows unless there's a reason.

## Lessons learned

- **Start Association early.** The worst advice so far was "Association doesn't advance the race" on turn one. Get a partner zoo or university in the first few turns and support the first conservation project by about conservation 2 to 5. A late first project (around turn 20) was judged the costliest call of that game.
- **Conservation wins.** Losses came from trailing on conservation while holding an appeal lead that couldn't cover it. Early conservation points also bring track bonuses and income.
- **Race for the project tiers that matter.** Holding a project for a tier the opponent then takes costs a lot; so does reaching five categories on Species Diversity second.
- **Open with cheap animals in standard enclosures.** A 10-coin aquarium for a 21-coin fish meant three or four actions per animal for the first third of the game.
- **Sponsors-for-cash turns are a trap.** Each one scores nothing and usually triggers the break. Andrew triggered 4 of 5 breaks in one game.
- **Get reputation up** so the third action upgrade arrives.
- **First upgrade**: Association or Animals before Sponsors, unless the plan is built on Sponsors income.
- **X-tokens over an unused worker** late in the game.
- **Don't take a card only to deny it** unless the opponent clearly wants it.
- **Drawing cards at a conservation milestone** has beaten taking coins.
- **Action efficiency beats best-move-per-turn.** Andrew's own orderings were often better: build before breaking for Sponsors money; Cards over Animals to avoid a costly discard; a project with better points per action over a low tier.
- **Combine Cards and Sponsors into one plan** (draw, then discard the unused cards for income) rather than judging each card alone.
- **Build own projects** (Yosemite did the job) when behind on conservation.
- **Final turn**: stack Association options (university, reputation, donation) for the last conservation points.
- **Drafting action cards**: community tier lists exist (TierMaker, Meeple Mountain); check them before recommending. On X-token-heavy maps the X-token trade Sponsors card is better than first credited.

## Game of Nov 26-28 2026 (Caves map, lost badly to The Professor)

Andrew judged his own play solid and Claude's advice the main cause of the loss. Claude's errors:

- **Swapped the Sponsors (@) and Association (person) icons** on the BGA action cards for several turns. Read the card name along the bottom, not the icon.
- **Said a placed animal can't move.** Building a reptile house, large bird aviary or aquarium lets you move a fitting animal into it from a standard enclosure.
- **Planned animals with no space to put them.** On the Caves map Andrew could only build size 2 or smaller (plus a petting zoo) until Build was upgraded. The Muriqui and Macaque sat in hand most of the game. **Check the map for a legal enclosure of the right size before recommending an animal or a build.**
- **Ignored the opponent's board for several turns** and missed his appeal jumping 9 to 21 and conservation reaching 10. Read both boards every turn.
- **Kept proposing plans Andrew had to correct**: playing the Research project for its bottom tier (it opens the 4- and 3-point tiers to the opponent; a project can only be supported once per player), cash turns that set up the opponent's Sponsors II break trigger, and a university he already had.
- **Card-order arithmetic was sloppy.** The used card goes to slot 1 and only the cards to its left shift up.

Lessons:

- **On a cramped map, the Build upgrade comes first.** Get the space before collecting big animals.
- **First conservation point came about 30% into the game; his came in the first third.** Same lesson as before: conservation early.
- **Hiring a second Association worker at strength 5** was Andrew's call and was right.
- **Before a cash Sponsors turn, check whether it puts the break within reach of the opponent's Sponsors II** (break X, gain 2X).
- **When the opponent leads appeal, break income favours him.** Stop pushing the break.
- **How the Professor won (his own words):** he released herbivores into the wild, each carrying +1 conservation, and snowballed into the conservation rewards. Strong players rate releasing highly. Claude never suggested it. Next game, check the release-into-the-wild projects from turn one and plan cheap animals that can be released.
- **His sponsor that rearranges enclosures** let him fix placement mistakes. Watch for sponsors like that.

## Strategy from strong players (researched 2026-11-28)

Sources: BGA Ark Nova tips page (en.doc.boardgamearena.com/Tips_arknova), BGA forum thread "I was 300 ELO for 300 games. Now I'm 500 ELO", Zatu tactics articles.

- **It's a sprint.** Gain appeal and conservation faster than the opponent, and end the game quickly. Don't over-optimize; play good animals, take breaks, repeat.
- **Releasing animals is a core engine.** Releasing costs the animal's appeal, pays conservation by its size, and **gives the enclosure back**. Value 1 conservation as about 3 appeal once past 10. Iconic animals (wolf, secretary bird, white stork, bison...) are the best releases. Cheap, low-appeal animals are good too, because what they pay in appeal roughly cancels out. Look for release projects from turn one, and draw toward them when the base projects don't fit your hand.
- **Support projects at the bottom tier early, across several projects.** Top tiers across several projects are hard to reach. For country projects, take 2 or 5 (4 gives only 3). For others, take 2 early or 4 or 5 later. First project by round 2 or 3. Claim project bonus spaces first, since they're removed once taken.
- **Conservation also raises income.** Every cube that leaves the income space pays out at breaks.
- **Upgrade priority:** Animals or Sponsors first, then Cards; Association is usually last. Build upgrades are overrated on many maps, because releases and size-2 bonuses free up enough space. (The exception is a cramped map like this game's Caves, where Build mattered.) The second worker from the conservation track is often better than upgrading Association. Sponsors upgrades are stronger than they look.
- **Claim a university or partner zoo early.** Asia is a solid default. Each matching continent icon cuts animal cost by 3.
- **Avoid small, low-appeal animals.** Go cheap, then medium, then expensive. Build speculative size-2 or 3 enclosures early.
- **Plan breaks.** Know whether a break helps you or the opponent (it pays more to the higher appeal). When you have no money and no workers, advancing the break is a fallback.
- **Watch the display and snap.** Snapping also denies the opponent. Don't dig for cards when you already have a playable option. With Cards upgraded, draw one card at a time.
- **Don't hoard cards.** A break can wipe out a big hand.
- **X-tokens:** a strength-5 action followed by a boosted strength-5 action makes a big burst.
- **Kiosks** earn 1 coin per adjacent building or occupied space. Place one or two next to many different structures, never in corners.
