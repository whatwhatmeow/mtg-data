# Brass: Lancashire

Played on Board Game Arena, 4 players. Compiled 2026-11-16 from the coaching chats of Oct 30 and Nov 10. First game: 3rd of 4 with 95 VP (winner 130).

## Before giving any advice

- Think in whole-game engines, not best move per turn. Andrew asked for this explicitly.
- Check each recommendation against what the other players are likely to do before confirming it.
- Read routes off the board. Don't assume a link exists.

## Rules Claude got wrong

- **You need an existing connection to network.** A canal can't be the first move with no network; build an industry first (the Lancaster port).
- **There is no direct Preston to Blackburn canal.** The route went through Wigan.
- **Some links are rail-only.** Claude called one canal-buildable when it wasn't.
- **Level I tiles don't survive into the Rail era.** Claude recommended a Cotton Mill I late in the Canal era; an Iron Works II, which carries over, was the right build.
- **Develop or Pass on the last turn scores nothing.** Use the final actions for points.

## Lessons learned

- **Industries pay when they flip** (sold or used up), not when built. Canals and level I tiles are removed at the end of the Canal era.
- **Engine that works**: coal mine, cotton mill, own port, sell. Add a shipyard for a large VP swing.
- **Grab shipyard spaces immediately.** Waiting one turn lost both Liverpool and Birkenhead.
- **Loans**: almost always the full £30, and only when the money funds a flip now or next turn. Loans permanently reduce income; idle cash is wasted.
- **Plan Rail-era development during the Canal era.** Develop away leftover level I tiles before the era ends (the last two Canal actions were used for this in game two) so Rail starts on level II.
- **Rails into Manchester and Liverpool** early in the Rail era. The winner's margin came from rail links into high-value towns and level III and IV tiles.
- **Don't connect something that opens a space for richer opponents.** Linking Barrow-in-Furness by rail would have exposed the shipyard space.
- Leave a combined cotton/port space open when placing a port, for flexibility later.
- Track opponents' spending to predict turn order and market prices.
